ChatGPT Remembers Everything You Type. Here Is How to Wipe It

Scroll down the left side of ChatGPT and you will see a record of your life in conversation form. There is the email you drafted last week, the cover letter from last month, the health question you asked at midnight, and the business idea you sketched out in January. It is all still there, neatly filed, ready to be reopened with a single click. That is convenient right up until the moment you share your screen in a meeting and a private conversation title flashes up for everyone to see, or until you lend your laptop to a family member and realise your entire chat history is one click away. ChatGPT remembers everything unless you tell it not to. The good news is that wiping that history is straightforward, once you know where the controls are hiding.

Why your chat history matters

Your ChatGPT history is more revealing than most people realise. It contains the questions you asked, the documents you pasted, the ideas you tested, and the personal details you shared along the way. If someone gains access to your account, they gain access to all of it. That could be a colleague who borrows your laptop, a family member using your computer, or a criminal who gets hold of your password. There is also the quieter issue of training: by default, OpenAI may use your conversations to improve its models. Deleting a conversation removes it from your account and, after a short retention period, from OpenAI's systems as well. Regular cleanup is one of the simplest privacy habits you can build, and it costs you nothing.

How to delete a single conversation

Deleting one chat is the easiest place to start. On a computer, hover over the conversation in the left sidebar, click the three dots that appear, and choose Delete. ChatGPT will ask you to confirm, and then the conversation is gone from your history. On the mobile app, swipe left on the conversation in your history list, or tap and hold it, then choose Delete. The process takes seconds. This is the right option when you have one or two chats you want gone: the health question, the job application, the personal email draft. Make it a habit to delete sensitive conversations as soon as you are finished with them, rather than letting them sit in the sidebar for months.

How to delete all of your conversations at once

If your history has been building up for months, deleting chats one by one is tedious. ChatGPT offers a bulk option. Go to Settings, then General, and look for the option to delete all chats. You will be asked to confirm, and then every conversation in your history is cleared at once. This is the nuclear option, and it is worth using if you have never cleaned up before. Before you do it, check whether there is anything you want to keep. You can export your data first from the same settings area, which downloads a copy of your conversations. That way you keep a personal archive while clearing the cloud copy. For most people, a fresh start every few months is a sensible rhythm.

What deleting actually does

It is worth being clear about what happens when you press delete. The conversation disappears from your account immediately, and you can no longer access it. OpenAI keeps deleted conversations for a short period, up to 30 days, for safety and abuse monitoring, and then removes them permanently. Deleted conversations are not used for training. That is the official policy, and it is a reasonable one, but it is worth understanding the nuance: deletion is not instant erasure from every server the second you click the button. It is a process that completes over days and weeks. That is normal for any online service. The practical point is that deleting is far better than leaving everything stored indefinitely, and the sooner you delete something sensitive, the sooner the clock starts.

How to stop ChatGPT saving your chats in the first place

Deleting history is reactive. The better approach is to stop the history from building up at all. ChatGPT offers temporary chats, which work like an incognito window for your browser. A temporary chat does not appear in your history, does not show up in your sidebar, and is not used for training. You can start one from the chat menu by selecting the temporary chat option. Use it for anything you would not want stored: personal questions, sensitive documents, or one-off queries you will never need again. The trade-off is that you cannot return to a temporary chat later, so do not use it for work you want to keep. For everything else, it is the most private way to use ChatGPT.

The training opt-out

Deleting history and stopping training are two different things, and you need both. Even if you delete your chats, conversations you had before changing the setting may already have been used for training. To stop future chats from being used, go to Settings, then Data Controls, and switch off "Improve the model for everyone". This stops OpenAI from using your future conversations to train its models. It does not delete anything you have already said, so combine it with a full history clear for a clean start. If you are on a team or enterprise plan, your data is not used for training by default, so this step is less urgent, but it is still worth checking the setting.

Memory: the feature that remembers across chats

ChatGPT has a memory feature that lets it remember facts about you across different conversations, such as your name, your preferences, and details you have mentioned before. This can be helpful: it means you do not have to repeat yourself. It can also be surprising: ask ChatGPT what it remembers about you, and you may be startled by how much it has stored. You can view and manage these memories in Settings under Personalization, where you can delete individual memories or turn the feature off entirely. If you value privacy over convenience, switching memory off is a sensible choice. If you keep it on, review the stored memories occasionally and delete anything you would rather it forgot.

Archiving versus deleting

ChatGPT also offers an archive option, which hides a conversation from your sidebar without deleting it. Archiving is useful for tidying up, but it is not a privacy tool. An archived conversation is still stored in your account, still appears in search, and can be restored at any time. If your goal is privacy, archiving does not help. Only deleting removes the conversation. Think of archiving as putting a file in a drawer and deleting as shredding it. Both have their place, but they are not the same thing, and confusing them is an easy mistake to make.

Clearing history on shared devices

If you use ChatGPT on a shared computer, a family tablet, or a work machine, your history is at greater risk. Always sign out when you finish, and avoid saving your password in a shared browser. Better still, use temporary chats on shared devices so nothing is stored in the first place. If you have already used ChatGPT on a device you no longer control, change your password and sign out of all sessions from your account settings. This is a quick step that closes the door on any device you have forgotten about. The bottom line

Your ChatGPT history is a diary you did not realise you were keeping. It is full of useful conversations, but it is also full of things you would probably not want a stranger to read. Deleting individual chats takes seconds, clearing everything takes a minute, and temporary chats let you avoid the problem entirely. Combine regular deletion with the training opt-out and a quick check of the memory settings, and you will have a ChatGPT account that works for you without keeping a permanent record of everything you have ever asked it. A little housekeeping now saves an awkward moment later.
